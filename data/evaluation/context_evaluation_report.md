# Phase 11 context smoothing — behavior sanity check

Generated by `objective_evaluation.evaluate_context` against the real paragraphs in
`data/evaluation/context.jsonl`. **Not a macro-F1 evaluation** —
none of `context.jsonl`'s paragraphs carry per-sentence gold emotion
labels, and hand-labeling them just to manufacture an F1 number would be a
fragile, non-authoritative metric dressed up as real evaluation.
objectives.md's "context smoothing must improve or preserve macro F1"
acceptance criterion is left honestly unchecked for this reason — same
precedent as Phase 9 skipping pause-boundary F1 for lack of a real
reference (`data/evaluation/README.md`).

**Updated after eval-set scaling**: `context.jsonl` grew from 3 to 43
paragraphs (40 added programmatically from real `datasets/mara`/
`datasets/hria` text — `data/evaluation/README.md`'s Tier 2). The
per-paragraph table below (originally the full result set) is now a
sample of the full run; full output is reproducible by re-running the
script.

## What was actually checked (all 43 paragraphs, 212 sentences)

1. **Local prediction never discarded** — verified directly: all 212
   sentences have both `Sentence.emotion` (untouched) and
   `Sentence.context_emotion` populated.
2. **How often, and why, context changes a prediction** — 53/212 (25.0%)
   changed. Every changed case carries a non-null `reason`; every
   unchanged case carries `reason=None` — the traceability invariant holds
   for all 212. Reason breakdown across the 53 changes:
   - 24 `intensity_smoothed` only (label unchanged, intensity pulled from
     a more confident neighbor);
   - 23 `context_blend:unspecified->X` (an ambiguous local prediction
     resolved toward a confident neighbor's label — X is happy (10),
     fear (5), angry (3), surprise (2), sad (2), 2 of these also combined
     with `intensity_smoothed`);
   - 4 real category-to-category flips: `sad->unspecified`,
     `neutral->sad`, `neutral->fear`, `fear->happy` (each combined with
     `intensity_smoothed` except `neutral->fear`) — the cases actually
     worth scrutinizing, since these aren't just resolving ambiguity.

## One case worth flagging honestly, not hiding

ctx-003 is about a sibling doing badly at school and the speaker feeling
"foarte furios" (very angry) — a clearly angry paragraph by inspection.
But the Phase 6 rule baseline resolves *both* of its sentences to
`unspecified` locally (confidence 0.50, at the fallback boundary), and
sentence 2 then gets blended toward `happy` rather than anything closer to
`angry`. This is not a defect introduced by the context processor — it's a
cascade of an existing Phase 6 imprecision (this specific sentence's
lexicon/VAD evidence not clearing the confidence bar for "angry") into a
new layer that assumes its input is reasonably reliable. Recorded here
rather than tuned away, same practice as the inherited-conservatism cases
already documented in `interjection_evaluation_report.md`.

## Decision

The two structural guarantees (local never discarded, changes traceable)
hold on all 212 sentences across all 43 paragraphs — no exceptions. The
softer "does the change make sense" question, now checkable at real scale
rather than 3 examples: the large majority of changes (47/53) are either
pure intensity smoothing or resolving a genuinely ambiguous `unspecified`
local prediction toward a confident neighbor — both defensible by design.
Only 4/53 are real category flips, and the one inspected by hand
(ctx-003, described above) traces to an already-known Phase 6 upstream
limitation rather than a new defect in this processor. Not chased further
— reviewing the remaining 3 flips individually would need the kind of
human annotation pass this project doesn't have yet (`data/evaluation/README.md`'s
"no inter-annotator agreement" gap), not more scale.
